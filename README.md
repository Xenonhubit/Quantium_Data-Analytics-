# Programme Overview
The main purpose of this virtual experience programme is to demonstrate how data analysts at Quantium leverage Python-based analytics to tackle complex retail challenges. Through this programme, I aim to showcase the practical application of customer analytics and experimental design in uncovering actionable insights from transactional retail datasets, closely mimicking real-world consulting scenarios for major supermarket chains.

#Case-Based Approach
This programme is designed around a realistic business case, where I work as an analyst evaluating chip category performance and customer purchasing behaviors for a supermarket client. By grounding the analysis in an authentic retail context—complete with customer segmentation frameworks and store trial evaluations—the programme makes data analytics methodologies and statistical testing techniques more relatable and applicable to actual category management challenges.

# Detailed Analysis
The programme is structured across three comprehensive tasks, each building upon the previous to create a complete retail analytics consulting experience. Each phase, from data preparation to commercial recommendations, is methodically documented to help others understand not only the "how" but also the "why" behind each analytical decision.

# Key insight
## Task 1: Customer Analytics
High-Value Segments Identified: Budget older families, Mainstream young singles/couples, and Mainstream retirees drive the highest total sales
Purchase Pattern Analysis: Older families purchase the largest average number of packets per customer, while mainstream young singles/couples represent the largest customer population
Product Preferences: Kettles chips and 175g packets dominate across most segments
Segment-Specific Behaviors: Mainstream young singles/couples show 28% higher affinity for Tyrells chips and 32% higher likelihood to purchase 270g Twisties packets compared to other segments
## Task 2: Store Trial Analysis
Control Store Matching: Used Pearson correlations and magnitude distance metrics to pair trial stores (77, 86, 88) with optimal control stores (233, 155, 40 respectively)
Statistical Testing: Applied hypothesis testing to evaluate performance differences between trial and control stores across total sales and customer count metrics
Trial Results: Stores 77 and 86 demonstrated statistically significant increases (above 95% confidence threshold) in sales and customer metrics during the trial period, while store 88 showed no significant change
## Task 3: Commercial Application
Synthesized insights from Tasks 1 and 2 into an executive presentation using the Pyramid Principle framework
Provided actionable recommendations based on customer segment preferences and trial store performance
